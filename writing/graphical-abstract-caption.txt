Temperature affects mammals' movement in a variety of ways, with cascading effects at the community level. For example, changes in temperature have direct effects of a caribou's movement behaviour (central red arrow), but they also have indirect effects through the changes in the behaviour of conspecifics, competitors, and predators. Changes in temperature may also alter the individuals' circadian rhythm and seasonal phenology as well as their habitat selection (e.g., selection for forest cover, elevation, water). Additionally, the effects of temperature on each of these can be nonlinear, as some species may move most at intermediate temperatures and less at extreme cold and heat).
